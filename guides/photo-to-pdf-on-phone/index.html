How to turn a photo into a PDF on your phone

By Layerbit, the makers of Foolscap · Updated 17 October 2026

Open Images to PDF in your phone’s browser, tap to choose photos from the gallery, and press Make a PDF. Then tap Send to send it to WhatsApp or email.

Images to PDF →Free. Nothing is uploaded.

How to do it

  1. Open Images to PDF in Chrome, Safari or any current phone browser.
  2. Tap the drop zone and choose the photos from your gallery.
  3. Drag them into order, or use the arrows.
  4. Press Make a PDF.
  5. Tap Share to send it, or Save to keep it in Downloads.

No app, and nothing uploaded

The PDF is made by your phone’s own browser. Your photos are not sent anywhere, which matters when they are of an ID card, a cheque or a medical report. You can add the site to your home screen to open it like an app; see PDF tools that work offline.

Taking the photo

If you are photographing a paper document, Scan gives a cleaner result: it straightens the page, removes the background and can make it black and white, which is also much smaller.

Sending it

On phones that can share files, Share hands the PDF straight to WhatsApp, Gmail or any other app. If a form needs it smaller, open it in Compress with the Then button and pick a size such as 200 KB.

On an Android phone

Open the site in Chrome or the phone’s own browser and open Images to PDF. Tapping the drop zone opens the phone’s picker, where you can choose from the gallery or from Files; in most pickers, a long press on one photo starts selecting several at once. After Make a PDF, Save puts the file in Downloads, and Share opens the list of apps that can take it.

On a slow mobile connection, it helps to open the site once on Wi-Fi. After that, the tools load from the phone itself, and making the PDF uses no mobile data at all, however many photos it holds.

On an iPhone

Open the site in Safari and open Images to PDF. Tapping the drop zone offers the Photo Library, the camera, or Files. Photos are usually handed over as JPEG; if one arrives in the HEIC format and cannot be read here, the tool says so, and setting Camera, Formats to Most Compatible fixes it for future photos. Saved PDFs usually land in the Files app, under Downloads.

A worked example

A college asks for your fee receipt and ID card as one PDF on WhatsApp. Open Images to PDF on your phone, add the two photos, check the order, and press Make a PDF. Tap Share, choose WhatsApp, and pick the chat. The PDF arrives as a document at full quality, rather than as photos that WhatsApp would squeeze. If the college sets a size limit instead, tap Then before sharing and choose Compress, with a size such as 500 KB.

Questions people ask

Does it work on a cheap Android phone?

Yes. It is built for low-end phones first. A large batch of photos just takes a little longer.

Where does the PDF go when I save it?

Into your phone’s Downloads, like any other download, unless you share it straight to an app instead.

Do I need to sign up?

No. There is no account, no sign-up and no login.

Can I add more photos later?

Make a new PDF with all of them, or make a PDF of the new photos and join the two with Merge.

Does it work without internet?

Yes, once the site has loaded. See PDF tools that work offline.

Why is my PDF so big?

Phone photos are large. Tap Then and choose Compress with a size such as 500 KB, or photograph paper with Scan in Black and white, which starts much smaller.
