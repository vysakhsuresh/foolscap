About Extract images

Pulls out every photo and picture stored inside a PDF, exactly as it is stored where that can be done: no re-compression, no loss. Each comes with its size and the page it came from. Drawings and text are not pictures, so they do not come out.

How to use it

  1. Add the PDF, typing its password if it asks.
  2. Press Pull the images out.
  3. Look through the list: each image gives its size in pixels, its type and the page it came from.
  4. Save the ones you want, or use Save all for a folder or one ZIP.

Good to know

This takes out the picture files that were put into the PDF, byte for byte. A JPEG photo comes out as the very same JPEG, often far larger in pixels than it looks on the page, because PDFs commonly shrink a big photo to fit a column. Nothing is redrawn, so nothing is lost.

Only real pictures come out. Charts, logos and diagrams drawn from lines and shapes are not pictures inside the file, and neither is text. A scanner normally stores each page as one picture, so a scanned PDF gives one image per page. Pictures stored in forms an ordinary image file cannot hold as they are, such as a colour palette, print CMYK, or black-and-white fax and JBIG2 scans, are decoded the way a PDF reader shows them and saved as PNG; the result line says which.

Pictures in a document someone sent you may belong to them or to someone else, so check before reusing them. To capture a page exactly as it looks, drawings and words included, use PDF to images instead.

Questions people ask

Why did a scanned PDF give one image per page?

Scanners usually store each page as one picture.

What is a .jp2 file?

JPEG 2000, a format some PDFs use. If your viewer cannot open it, PDF to images gives the page as a normal JPEG.

Why does an image look different from the page?

The page may show only part of the picture, or a smaller copy of it. You get the whole picture as it was stored.
