How to convert JPG images to a PDF

By Layerbit, the makers of Foolscap · Updated 17 October 2026

Open Images to PDF, add your pictures, put them in order and press Make a PDF. Each image becomes one page.

Images to PDF →Free. Nothing is uploaded.

How to do it

  1. Open Images to PDF and add the images.
  2. Drag the pictures into page order, or use the arrows.
  3. Choose a page size: A4, Letter, or Same as each image.
  4. Choose a margin: none, small or large.
  5. Press Make a PDF and save it.

Which images work

JPEG, PNG, WebP, GIF and BMP all work. iPhone HEIC photos work where the browser can read them; where it cannot, the tool says so and how to share them as JPEG instead. Photos taken sideways on a phone come out the right way up.

Page size

A4 suits anything that will be printed or uploaded to an Indian form: each picture is fitted onto an A4 page. Same as each image makes every page the shape of its picture, which suits screenshots and posters.

Photos of paper

If the pictures are photos of documents, Scan does more: it finds the sheet’s corners, straightens it and cleans it up so it looks scanned. To add the result to other PDFs, use Merge; to meet a size limit, use Compress.

A worked example

You have five photos of handwritten class notes to send as one file. Open Images to PDF, add the five photos, and use the arrows so the first page is at the top. Choose A4 and a Small margin, and press Make a PDF. The result line says five images on A4. If the photos are of paper, try the same photos in Scan too: it straightens them and whitens the paper, which reads better and makes a smaller file.

Margins and borders

None puts each picture right to the edge of the page, which suits photos and posters. Small leaves a narrow white border, which looks tidy when printed and keeps the picture clear of the printer’s own margin, where many printers cannot print. Large leaves room to write notes or punch holes for a file. With Same as each image, a margin puts a white border around the picture at its own shape.

If you are not sure, A4 with a Small margin is a safe choice for anything that might be printed or uploaded to a form, and Same as each image with None is best for anything only ever read on a screen.

Common mistakes

Adding photos in the gallery’s order without checking: galleries often sort newest first, which puts the last page at the top. Putting a wide screenshot on A4, where it ends up small in the middle of a tall page; Same as each image keeps each one full size. And sending a 20 MB PDF of phone photos to a form that wants 1 MB: tap Then and Compress it to the limit.

Questions people ask

How many images can I add?

As many as you like. There is no limit beyond your device’s memory.

Are my photos uploaded?

No. The PDF is put together in your browser, and the photos stay on your device.

Does it make the photos blurry?

No. JPEG and PNG pictures go in exactly as they are. WebP, BMP and HEIC pictures, and photos that need turning upright, are saved again as high-quality JPEGs, and GIFs as PNGs.

The PDF is too big for a form. What now?

Tap Then on the result and choose Compress, then pick a size such as 200 KB. See compress to 200 KB.

Can I make a PDF from PNG screenshots?

Yes. PNG pictures go in exactly as they are, and Same as each image keeps each page the shape of the screen.

Can I make one PDF per photo?

Yes. Tick A separate PDF for each image: every photo becomes its own PDF, named after it, in one go.
